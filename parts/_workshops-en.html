What I do

Workshops on AI, startups, creative practice, communication, and confidence.

Where I come from

I ran my first workshop when I was 14. It was about making and playing with flowersticks, a passion I’ve enjoyed almost my entire life. Since then, I passed the Cambridge CELTA teaching certificate with a Pass A (top 5% of candidates), took part in more than a dozen Erasmus+ projects, ran a Language Club in Jordan (our meetings were sometimes interrupted by bomb sirens), and learned that teaching is what I do best and where I can make the biggest difference.

International workshops I’ve attended across Europe have been central to my development: practising languages in real settings, working with groups in unfamiliar contexts, building international networks, and learning to operate comfortably across cultures. I’ve attended many workshops, from counterproductive to life-changing, and I’ve learned what works, and for whom. I love teaching and learning, and I’ve had the chance to run sessions on AI, startups, and creative practice.

Today I’m an English teacher and translator, a juggler and the founder of Learntrain. Since 2026, I’ve worked with Fundacja One Europe. I build my workshops on my own experience: I’ve run a startup, built an AI tool, and spent years performing on stage in various countries. I don’t teach what I haven’t tested myself.

How I teach

I run practical, modern workshops. I make sure all the information is up to date and actionable. I show the best tools and practices. I present theory when necessary, but prefer participants to find information on their own, ideally through practice (for example, using AI to learn about the technology behind it). Just like in my language lessons, I make sure learners understand WHY a particular piece of information is important; otherwise it goes in one ear and out the other.

I believe people tend to forget PowerPoint presentations, but they remember action, the surprises, the unique things: the tips and tricks they immediately recognise as useful. I focus on non-formal education methods and learning through doing, because this is how I’ve learned most effectively.

What it looks like in practice

Here’s a taste of how I work. During my workshops I’ve asked participants to:

  • Come up with a real problem they face, find a solution, and publish a website with a sign-up form to check interest in their startup idea.
  • Pretend to be a toxic partner to see whether an AI chatbot will always be “on their side”, and how this behaviour differs between models, in order to understand the risk of using chatbots as therapists or advisers.
  • Use provided frameworks to boost originality and create a juggling move nobody has done before.
  • Try to jailbreak an AI model into giving them potentially dangerous information, in order to understand the harmful potential of this technology and how modern guardrails work.

And many more. Some of the workshops are a bit scary or emotional, sometimes funny, often all of these. I think emotions are the core of memorability! I’ve been very lucky to have truly amazing participants, and I often end up learning something as well. I believe seeing the different approaches is what makes running workshops in intercultural settings truly eye-opening.
